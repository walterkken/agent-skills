# Abstract Template A: Challenge to Contribution

Use this pattern when readers can understand the proposed solution directly from the problem.

1. Define the input, output, or scientific question.
2. Identify the particular limitation that the work addresses.
3. Name the contribution and explain its role in one or two sentences.
4. Connect that contribution to a benefit that the results can support.
5. End with the central measured finding and its scope.

```latex
% Task and setting: [what is studied, using what evidence]
% Gap: [specific limitation and why it matters]
% Contribution: [proposed change and the obstacle it addresses]
% Benefit: [mechanism connecting the change to its expected effect]
% Result: [verified measurement, comparator, and conditions]
```

Draft the result from supplied evidence; a template cannot establish novelty or performance.
