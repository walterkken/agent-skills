# Avoid an Introduction That Hides the Method

A method introduction should let readers understand what is done. New terminology and statements of intent cannot replace the essential operations.

The local notes discuss an example with separate assessments of image realism and pose consistency. The useful editing question is whether the paragraph explains the inputs, targets, and interaction of those assessments. Naming branches and losses alone does not answer that question.

Review the paragraph with four checks:

1. Can a reader identify the system's inputs and outputs?
2. Does each component perform a concrete, stated operation?
3. Is the training or decision signal explained?
4. Is each expected benefit connected to the mechanism that could produce it?

Revise vague claims into an ordered account of the process. Keep genuine technical simplicity visible; novelty should rest on an attributable contribution and supporting evidence.
