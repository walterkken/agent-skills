# Research Evidence Auditor

Build an auditable chain from every conclusion to the source evidence. Separate what a source states from what the analysis infers.

## Establish scope

1. Restate the research question and define the unit of analysis.
2. Inventory supplied sources before searching for more.
3. Record date, population, setting, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcome, and study design when relevant.
4. State material scope limits. Do not silently generalize beyond the evidence.

## Build a claim ledger

Atomize compound assertions into independently checkable claims. Assign stable IDs (`C01`, `C02`).

For each claim, record:

- exact claim or quotation;
- source and precise locator;
- source type and authority;
- verification result;
- supporting or conflicting evidence;
- confidence and rationale.

Read [references/audit-framework.md](references/audit-framework.md) before producing a formal audit, evidence table, contradiction analysis, or gap map.

## Verify citations

1. Open the cited source rather than relying on search snippets or secondary descriptions.
2. Locate the exact supporting passage, table, figure, dataset field, or result.
3. Check identity: author, title, venue, year, version, DOI or URL.
4. Check fidelity: quotation accuracy, paraphrase meaning, numbers, units, denominator, direction, and uncertainty.
5. Check entailment: distinguish direct support, partial support, contextual support, and no support.
6. Check scope: population, geography, period, experimental conditions, endpoints, and causal versus correlational language.
7. Prefer primary sources. Use secondary sources to find leads, not as substitutes when the primary source is available.
8. Mark inaccessible or unverified material explicitly. Never invent a locator or imply access that did not occur.

## Detect patterns

Normalize comparable evidence before synthesis. Group results only when constructs, measurements, and contexts are sufficiently aligned.

Look for:

- repeated direction or magnitude;
- dose, time, subgroup, or geographic gradients;
- clusters by method, dataset, funding, or publication period;
- recurring mechanisms or failure modes;
- notable negative or null results.

Report the observations that generate each pattern and plausible alternative explanations. Do not count repeated citations to the same underlying dataset as independent replication.

## Analyze contradictions

Treat disagreement as a testable object, not a rhetorical label.

1. Pair the conflicting claims and cite both.
2. Decide whether the conflict is direct, apparent, methodological, temporal, definitional, or contextual.
3. Compare population, intervention or exposure, comparator, outcome definition, timeframe, design, sample size, model, exclusions, and uncertainty.
4. State whether a boundary condition reconciles the findings.
5. Leave the contradiction unresolved when evidence cannot adjudicate it.

## Map evidence gaps

Distinguish:

- **coverage gap**: relevant population, setting, variable, or period is absent;
- **quality gap**: evidence exists but is biased, indirect, underpowered, or poorly measured;
- **consistency gap**: credible findings conflict;
- **mechanism gap**: an association lacks a tested explanation;
- **replication gap**: a result depends on one team, dataset, or method;
- **reporting gap**: missing methods, denominators, uncertainty, or negative results;
- **access gap**: potentially relevant evidence could not be inspected.

For every gap, explain what evidence would reduce it and why that evidence is decision-relevant. Absence of located evidence is not proof that no evidence exists.

## Report

Lead with a concise answer calibrated to evidence strength. Then provide:

1. scope and method;
2. claim ledger or citation audit;
3. supported patterns;
4. contradiction matrix;
5. evidence-gap map;
6. prioritized next searches or studies;
7. limitations and access constraints.

Use direct links and precise locators. Label observations, source-reported interpretations, and analyst inferences. Use confidence terms consistently and avoid false numerical precision.
