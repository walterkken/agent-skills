# Technical Challenge Version 1: An Established Task

Build a sequence of approaches in which each limitation explains why the next approach was developed.

For each selected method family, describe its relevant mechanism, what it achieves, and the condition under which the unresolved problem appears. Finish with the limitation that the present study addresses. Avoid turning this sequence into a chronological catalogue.

The local pose-estimation example moves through correspondence methods, direct prediction, and intermediate keypoint representations. Its teaching purpose is to show that a newer approach may resolve one problem while retaining another, such as difficulty when the required observations are hidden.

```latex
% Shared difficulty: [source of ambiguity or missing information]
% Approach A: [mechanism] addresses [part of that difficulty].
% Remaining problem: [limitation tied to A's assumptions]
% Approach B: [mechanism] changes [relevant assumption or representation].
% Target gap: [specific unresolved condition motivating this work]
```

Check each factual comparison against the papers actually cited in the manuscript. Teaching-source links are recorded in the [example bank index](../index.md).
